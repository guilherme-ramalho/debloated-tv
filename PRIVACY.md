# Privacy Policy

Purio TV (formerly Debloated TV) · effective 15 November 2026

Purio TV collects no personal data. It has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no servers of its own.

## What the app does with data

- **Talks only to your TV.** All connections go from your iPhone to the TV on your local network: the TV's remote
  service, its network debugging when you run TV setup, and the Home screen editor. Nothing is sent to the developer
  or to any third party.
- **Keeps its settings on your iPhone.** The TV's address and name, the pairing keys (in the iOS Keychain), your
  remote shortcuts, quick actions, Home screen layouts and the recovery records made by TV setup are stored on the
  device only. Deleting the app deletes them; a recovery record you export goes wherever you save it.
- **Photos stay between your iPhone and your TV.** A wallpaper you choose is sent to the TV and nowhere else; the app
  only sees the photos you select for it. Show on TV reads your photo library on the iPhone (only the photos you allow,
  if you limit access) and sends the photo or video you open to your TV over your home network, through the TV's own
  Cast player. Photos go without their location or camera details. Nothing is uploaded anywhere else.
- **Local network access** is used to find and connect to your TV. iOS asks for it the first time. While the
  Connect screen is open, the app lists the TVs that announce themselves on your network and briefly opens a
  connection to a few of their ports to tell what kind of TV each one is (Android TV, Roku, LG, Samsung, Chromecast).
  No data is sent in those checks, nothing is kept after you leave the screen, and nothing leaves your network.
- **Siri and Shortcuts.** Phrases are handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; the app only receives the action
  to run.

## On the TV

TV setup turns off apps you choose and can install an open-source Home screen. That Home screen shows the weather
only if you turn it on. The TV then uses its own approximate location (Android's network location, from nearby
Wi-Fi networks), rounds it to about 1 km, and asks Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) for the forecast there, about every
30 minutes while the Home screen is showing. The TV may also look up the town's name through Android's location
service. Your iPhone's location is never used, and the TV sends no other data. Turning the weather off stops all of
this.

## Crash reports

If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in iOS Settings, Apple may send anonymous crash reports.
The app adds nothing to them.

## Children

The app does not collect data from anyone, including children.
